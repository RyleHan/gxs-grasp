\begin{abstract}
Language-driven grasp detectors condition the whole network on the instruction, although the
instruction mainly decides \emph{where} to grasp; width and angle follow from the object's
shape. We therefore split grasp quality into two maps. The \emph{graspability} map $G$ is
a GR-ConvNet that never sees the text, trained on the union of all grasps annotated in an image.
The \emph{selection} map $S$ compares frozen dense CLIP features with the instruction and only has to choose
among graspable regions. Both maps are trained with the existing Grasp-Anything++ labels, because almost
every image has several instructions whose grasps serve as negatives for each other. With the same grasp
backbone and training data, \ours improves the success rate of an additive GR-ConvNet\,+\,CLIP baseline
from 45.5\% to 59.6\% on seen and from 48.3\% to 58.9\% on unseen categories, and more than doubles
the rate at which both objects of a pair are grasped.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
To \emph{``grip the keychain by its ring''}, a robot has to find the object the sentence refers to
and decide how to close a parallel gripper on it. The public release of
Grasp-Anything++~\cite{vuong2024lgd} contains about 0.8M synthetic scenes with 4.4M such instructions,
and detectors trained on it condition the whole network on the text~\cite{vuong2024lgd,vo2024maskgrasp}.

Two observations about this data motivate our design. First, the opening and angle of a grasp at a
pixel are set by the object's shape, so the text only needs to pick the location. Second, an
image comes with one instruction for each annotated object. Their grasps together cover the graspable
regions of these objects, and each instruction refers to only some of them. \ours therefore predicts
$Q=G\cdot S$. We contribute a factorised detector whose geometry heads never see
the text, a sibling-contrast loss that trains selection without mask labels, and a paired metric and
oracle analysis suggesting that most errors come from selection.

\section{Related Work}
CLIPort~\cite{shridhar2021cliport} fuses CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} features into dense pick
affordances, and CROG~\cite{tziafas2023crog} adds grasp maps to CLIP-based referring
segmentation~\cite{wang2022cris}. On Grasp-Anything++~\cite{vuong2024lgd}, an extension
of Grasp-Anything~\cite{vuong2024graspanything}, LGD treats grasp detection as conditional
diffusion, and mask-guided methods~\cite{vo2024maskgrasp,bhat2025maplegrasp} use object masks to focus
the grasp features. We instead combine two branches only at the output, keep the geometry
free of language and use no mask labels.

\section{Method}
Given an image $I$ and an instruction $\ell$, we predict a grasp $g=(x,y,w,h,\theta)$.
\para{Graspability.}A GR-ConvNet~\cite{kumra2020grconvnet} maps $I$ to five $224{\times}224$ maps:
quality logits $q$, $\cos2\theta$, $\sin2\theta$, opening $w$ and jaw size $h$. None of them depends
on $\ell$. If an image has instructions $\ell_{1..K}$ with grasp sets $\mathcal{G}_{1..K}$, we draw the
targets from $\bigcup_k\mathcal{G}_k$ and minimise
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_G=\mathrm{BCE}(q,\hat q)+\textstyle\sum_{m}\big\lVert (m-\hat m)\odot\hat q\big\rVert_{\text{sL1}},
\end{equation}
where $\hat q$ marks the centre third of each rectangle and $m$ runs over the geometry maps. Computing the
geometry loss only on grasp pixels was necessary: with GR-ConvNet's dense regression, the predicted
opening stayed below 15\,px on the scenes we checked (labels: 73--89\,px), even when overfitting 70 scenes.

\para{Selection.}We run a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 at $448{\times}448$ and take $28{\times}28$ patch features
$v_p$ from the value path of its last block~\cite{zhou2022maskclip,lan2024clearclip}. With text embedding $t$ and
zero-initialised residual adapters $\phi,\psi$,
\begin{equation}
S(p\mid\ell)=\sigma\big(\tau\langle\phi(v_p),\psi(t)\rangle+b\big).
\end{equation}
For $\ell_k$, patches covered by $\mathcal{G}_k$ are positives and patches covered by a sibling
$\mathcal{G}_{j\ne k}$ are negatives. The loss is class-balanced BCE; other patches
are ignored, as $G$ suppresses them.

\para{Inference.}$Q=\sigma(q)\cdot S$ with $S$ upsampled. The grasp centre is the maximum of $Q$
after Gaussian smoothing; $\theta,w,h$ are read from $G$ there (Fig.~\ref{fig:qual}).

\section{Experiments}
\para{Data.}We test on the official LGD splits and keep the 515 seen and 212 unseen objects
whose instructions are released (1{,}376 and 601 instructions). The official training split has
8{,}374 images with released instructions, but only 1{,}668 of them list two or more objects. We keep
the 8{,}122 of them that pass our filters and add 12{,}000 multi-object scenes from the full release;
the filters remove test scenes, object names outside the training vocabulary and duplicate names. This gives 20{,}122 training images (118{,}292
instructions) and 500 validation images. In 80.1\% of part pairs of the same object the grasps are
identical, and our subset has only 2{,}855 distinct sentences, so the instruction mostly names
the object.

\para{Metrics.}A grasp succeeds if its IoU with a ground-truth grasp of the instruction exceeds 0.25
and its angle error is below $30^\circ$~\cite{vuong2024lgd}. On multi-object test images we also
report \emph{paired} (both grasps of an object pair succeed) and \emph{select} (predicted centre
inside the target's ground-truth rectangles).

\para{Setup.}$G$ and the additive baseline (same backbone, CLIP text feature added at the bottleneck)
are trained for 12 epochs (Adam, $10^{-3}$, cosine, batch 32), and $S$ for 4 epochs from
zero-shot CLIP, in about two hours on a free Colab T4.

\para{Results.}The additive baseline is 5.6 points above $G$ alone (Table~\ref{tab:main}), while
multiplying the same $G$ by a selection map adds 19.7. The baseline uses CLIP only for the
text, whereas $S$ also uses its image encoder, so this comparison does not isolate the factorisation.
Zero-shot CLIP already reaches 55.3\%, and the sibling-contrast loss adds 4.3 points. Using all
background patches as negatives lowers accuracy, also when we rank the objects of
validation images by $S$ (66.3\% zero-shot, 64.6\% all patches, 71.1\% ours). Restricting $G$
to the ground-truth rectangles gives 88.9\%, so most remaining errors come from selection. The improvement also holds for unseen categories, probably
because $S$ inherits CLIP's vocabulary, and for images with a single annotated object (40.7\% for $G$
alone, 60.2\% for \ours), which likely contain unlabelled objects that $G$ finds graspable. We do not list published results, which use other training data.

\section{Conclusion}
Grasp geometry can be learned from all annotated grasps of an image, and selection from the contrast
between sibling instructions, without extra labels. A limitation is the $28{\times}28$ CLIP grid, which
is too coarse for small parts, although the current labels rarely distinguish parts.
